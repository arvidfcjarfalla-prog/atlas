# Common Mistakes (failure modes)

What kills threads, with the evidence. Sources in `sources.md`. Three of these
are tactics that *sound* smart but were actively **refuted** in research — do
not use them.

## Mistakes backed by data

### 1. The Question Deficit — not asking enough
Most daters think they ask enough questions; far fewer feel their *dates* ask
enough of them. Hinge's 2025 D.A.T.E. Report: **85% are more likely to want a
second date when asked thoughtful questions.** Under-asking is the most common,
most fixable failure. → ask genuine, specific questions. [Hinge 2025]

### 2. Talking about yourself instead of them
Openers heavy on "I" and light on "you" get fewer replies. More second-person
("you") signals real attention. The lever is recipient-focus, not self-pitch.
[Schöndienst & Dang-Xuan 2011]

### 3. Writing long messages to "impress" a desirable match
People write up to **twice as long** to more desirable prospects — and it
**mostly doesn't work** (paid off for exactly one subgroup in the data). Effort ≠
length. Relevance and recipient-focus are the levers. [Bruch & Newman 2018]

### 4. "Punching up" hard and expecting replies
Reply probability drops markedly as the desirability gap widens; reply rates to
the most-desired targets cap around ~21%. Aspirational messaging has a real,
quantified cost — calibrate, and don't read low reply rates as personal failure.
[Bruch & Newman 2018]

### 5. Chatting forever / never asking out (and asking too early)
There's an **inverted-U**: a *brief-to-moderate* text phase produces the best
in-person outcomes; both too little and too much messaging hurt. Endless texting
builds an idealized image that deflates on meeting. Move to a date after a brief
phase (~roughly 1–3 weeks), not after weeks of limbo. [Ramirez et al. 2015]

### 6. Treating humor as a guaranteed opener
Humor is **conditional**, not universal: it helps with moderately attractive
matches but can backfire with highly attractive ones (halo effect). Humor is
seasoning, not a formula. [Tilburg 2017; note: medium-confidence]

## Refuted tactics — DO NOT put these in advice

> These three were checked and **failed** verification. They're popular myths.

- ❌ **"Be implicit / never state interest directly."** Implicitness ("imagined
  togetherness") is *one available strategy*, but the claim that flirting is
  *primarily* implicit was refuted (0-3). Direct, genuine interest is valid and
  often better. [refuted vs Mortensen 2017]
- ❌ **"Reply faster and keep messages short to boost engagement."** The claim
  that quicker/shorter replies raise engagement was refuted (0-3). Don't
  optimize for speed/brevity as a tactic. [refuted vs Zhang & Yasseri 2016]
- ❌ **"Humor reliably makes you more attractive."** The positive *main effect*
  of humor was refuted (1-2). See mistake #6 — it's conditional. [refuted vs
  Tilburg 2017]

## The classic openers that underperform
- "hey" / "hi" / "how's your day" — generic, no hook, easy to ignore.
- Looks-only or sexual openers to a stranger — creepy, low reply.
- Interview mode — question after question with zero self-disclosure.
- Copy-paste lines that scream template (now also "AI-sounding" polish).
- Persistence after silence or a no — not a mistake, a boundary violation.
