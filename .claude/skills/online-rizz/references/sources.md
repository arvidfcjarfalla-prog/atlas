# Sources

From a deep-research run (6 search angles, 18 sources fetched, 47 claims
extracted, 25 verified with 3-vote adversarial verification → 22 confirmed, 3
refuted). Quality is split: academic sources are the strongest evidence but
mostly **correlational/descriptive**, not experimental; Hinge Labs figures are
**brand self-report surveys** (directional, not proof). Several datasets are
2011–2016 and predate current app dynamics (Bumble women-first, voice prompts,
AI-assisted messaging) — treat base-rate numbers as era/app-specific.

## Primary / academic (strongest)

- **Bruch & Newman (2018)** — "Aspirational pursuit of mates in online dating
  markets," *Science Advances*. ~187k users, 4 US cities. Desirability gap
  lowers reply rates; longer messages to desirable targets mostly don't pay off.
  https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.aap9815

- **Ramirez, Sumner, Fleuriet & Cole (2015)** — "When Online Dating Partners
  Meet Offline," *Journal of Computer-Mediated Communication* 20(1). Inverted-U:
  meet after a *brief* online phase, not an extended one.
  https://academic.oup.com/jcmc/article/20/1/99/4067565

- **Huang & Hancock (2022)** — "Will You Go on a Date with Me? Predicting First
  Dates from Linguistic Traces…," *J. Language and Social Psychology* 41(4)
  (Stanford Social Media Lab). Five interpersonal processes: investment,
  interdependence, emotional dynamics, decision-making, coordination.
  https://www.sciencegate.app/document/10.1177/0261927x211066612

- **Schöndienst & Dang-Xuan (2011)** — "The Role of Linguistic Properties in
  Online Dating Communication," PACIS. 167,276 contact-initiation messages.
  Fewer "I", more "you" → more replies.
  https://www.researchgate.net/publication/221229185

- **Mortensen (2017)** — "Flirting in online dating: …flirtatious implicitness,"
  *Discourse Studies* 19(5). "Imagined togetherness" + avoiding closure as *one*
  flirting strategy (not the only register).
  https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1461445617715179

- **Zhang & Yasseri (2016)** — "What Happens After You Both Swipe Right,"
  arXiv:1607.03320 (Oxford Internet Institute). ~2M conversations. Men initiate
  ~79%; ~49% of conversations unreciprocated; phone exchange usually within first
  20 messages. Era/app-specific (pre-Bumble-model).
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/1607.03320

- **Tilburg (2017)** — "Humorous at Heart: Opening Lines and Physical
  Attractiveness on Tinder," master's thesis, N=243. Humor is conditional on the
  match's attractiveness. *Medium confidence (unpublished, hypothetical
  profiles).*
  https://arno.uvt.nl/show.cgi?fid=156121

## Institutional / expert

- **Hinge Labs — 2025 Gen Z D.A.T.E. Report** (~30k daters). The 85% thoughtful-
  questions stat and the Question Deficit. Brand self-report.
  https://hinge.co/newsroom/2025-GenZ-Report

- **Hinge — "10 Lessons in Love"** (newsroom). Voice Prompts 32% more likely to
  lead to a date (first-party, methodology undisclosed → directional only).
  https://hinge.co/newsroom/love-lessons

- **Logan Ury** — Director of Relationship Science at Hinge; behavioral
  scientist (Harvard, ex-Google "Irrational Lab"); author *How to Not Die
  Alone*. The ethical, evidence-informed anchor for this skill.
  https://www.loganury.com/ · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logan_Ury

## Refuted in verification (do NOT cite as advice)

- "Flirting is *primarily* implicit" — refuted 0-3 (vs Mortensen 2017).
- "Faster/shorter replies raise engagement" — refuted 0-3 (vs Zhang & Yasseri).
- "Humor has a positive *main* effect on attractiveness/intent" — refuted 1-2.

## Open questions (not yet well-evidenced)

- Bumble (women-message-first) and LGBTQIA+ contexts — much data is
  heterosexual-only.
- Whether findings hold post-2023 in the AI-assisted-opener era.
- The exact *wording* of the ask-out (literature covers timing, not phrasing).
- Head-to-head reply rates of opener archetypes (profile-ref vs question vs
  tease).
